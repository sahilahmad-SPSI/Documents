Week ending 2 September 2026

1

Propeller-strike crash detection

Now active by default in both flight worlds

Previously the aircraft could not crash. An impact stopped it dead and it carried on flying, which was unrealistic and made collision behaviour impossible to demonstrate. Prop-strike behaviour existed, but only inside a separate test world that had to be selected deliberately.

It is now active in normal flight. When a propeller's swept disc contacts structure — the chimney, a building or the ground — with the propeller turning, all four rotors stop. Thrust is lost, the aircraft falls, and the autopilot disarms itself, reporting Crash: Disarming.

The trigger requires the propeller to be turning above 150 rad/s, roughly a third of hover speed, and the contact to persist for three consecutive physics steps. Those two conditions distinguish a genuine strike from a light graze or a propeller that is merely windmilling.

Two questions had to be answered: does it fire when it should, and does it stay silent when it should. Both flight worlds were tested this week.

Scope of the model

The propellers stop and the aircraft falls. No structural damage is modelled — the airframe and propellers remain intact on the ground, because the physics engine provides no means of representing a component breaking. What is simulated is the consequence of a strike, not the damage itself, and no conclusion about surviving a real impact should be drawn from the aircraft appearing undamaged.

Recovery after a crash is a single command, returning the simulation to a flight-ready state in approximately 20 seconds.

2

Onboard forward camera (FPV)

Aimed, verified and publishing live

The aircraft carries a camera on the nose deck looking forward along the airframe, published live throughout the flight. It provides the view an inspection pilot would work from, and it is the view in which a chimney contact is actually judged.

Framing

The camera position was selected against three stated criteria rather than aimed by eye, and each was verified by projecting the aircraft geometry into the camera frame:

  • the orange-tipped UT probe sits low in the frame, keeping the contact point visible throughout a chimney touch;
  • the horizon sits in the upper half, preserving attitude reference for the pilot;
  • the landing gear is kept clear of the centre of the image.

The camera is mounted 180 mm forward and 250 mm above the aircraft centre, pitched 2° below horizontal — close to level, because the probe disc sits high on the airframe.

3

New plant world

Detailed inspection site, flight-tested

A considerably more detailed representation of the inspection site is now available. It contains 12 models and 78 collision bodies, compared with 2 and 1 in the world used to date — the same site, with the 45 m striped chimney, the buildings and the gas holder, but with real geometry throughout rather than a largely bare ground plane.

It has been flown this week: a complete camera lap end to end, recorded clean, and a deliberate propeller strike within it to confirm crash detection behaves the same there as in the established world.

Which world is used for what

WorldRole
gen5_plant_v3 Demonstration. The detailed site, for presentation and visual review.
gen5_plant Validation. Every measured and reported result was flown here, so it remains the reference world for any figure that must stand up to scrutiny.

Both worlds now carry crash detection and the forward camera, so the two differ only in the level of site detail.

Everything below was run on this machine before it was written down. Open a terminal and work top to bottom — the first block is needed once per session, the rest is per flight.

Step 0

Set up the terminal

cd ~/gz_ws/src/GazebooAssets export GZ_SIM_RESOURCE_PATH="$PWD/Models/converted:$PWD/Gen5Flight/models:$PWD/InspectionYard/exports:$GZ_SIM_RESOURCE_PATH" export GZ_SIM_SYSTEM_PLUGIN_PATH="$HOME/gz_ws/build:$PWD/MagnetPlugin/build:$GZ_SIM_SYSTEM_PLUGIN_PATH"

The second line is what lets the crash detector load. Without it the world still opens, but a prop strike does nothing.

The quick way

One command for the whole stack

# the new showcase world Gen5Flight/scripts/sim_stack.sh reset --world gen5_plant_v3.sdf # or the validated flight world (the default) Gen5Flight/scripts/sim_stack.sh reset

It opens the world and the autopilot in their own windows, waits until both are genuinely ready, and prints READY TO FLY. Use the same command after a crash.

Gen5Flight/scripts/sim_stack.sh status # what is running Gen5Flight/scripts/sim_stack.sh stop # shut it down cleanly
The manual way

Load the world and start the simulation

  1. Start the world — terminal 1

    # the new detailed world gz sim -r Gen5Flight/worlds/gen5_plant_v3.sdf # the validated one gz sim -r Gen5Flight/worlds/gen5_plant.sdf

    Wait until the drone and the striped chimney are on screen.

  2. Start the autopilot — terminal 2

    Gen5Flight/scripts/start_sitl.sh

    Wait for the STABILIZE> prompt.

  3. Wind, if you want it — terminal 3

    python3 Gen5Flight/scripts/wind_panel.py

    Tick Enable wind, set speed and direction, press Apply. Wind takes about 30 seconds to arrive.

    Every time the world restarts

    Restarting switches the wind off. The panel tells you in red, but it does not come back on its own — press Apply again.

Testing the camera

Put the FPV view on screen

In the Gazebo window:

  1. open the ⋮ menu, top right;
  2. search for Image Display and add it;
  3. choose the topic /gen5/fpv.

The orange-tipped probe should sit in the lower centre of the picture with the horizon up in the top half. To save a single frame to a file instead:

python3 Gen5Flight/scripts/grab_fpv.py ~/fpv_shot.png
Testing the crash

Make it hit the chimney on purpose

This flies the aircraft into the chimney side-on so a propeller disc meets the steel, then records what happened.

# bring up the crash stack on the flight world bash PropStrikePlugin/scripts/start_stack.sh "$PWD/Gen5Flight/worlds/gen5_plant.sdf" # fly the strike — chimney is at N 40, E 25 PROPSTRIKE_WORLD=gen5_plant python3 PropStrikePlugin/scripts/crash_battery.py strike \ /tmp/strike.json --target 40,25 --radius 2.0 --speed 4.0 --alt 20 --yaw-offset -90 # tidy up bash PropStrikePlugin/scripts/stop_stack.sh

You should see Crash: Disarming in the output, and "latched": true in /tmp/strike.json. Swap the target to 0,25 with --radius 7.35 --alt 5 to hit the building instead.

Why side-on

--yaw-offset -90 turns the aircraft so the propellers lead. A nose-first approach will not trigger: the forward UT probe contacts first and holds the propellers clear of the surface, exactly as the real aircraft is designed to. That is correct behaviour rather than a fault, and it is worth knowing before running the test.

Check nothing false-triggers

# three take-offs and landings plus ground handling — nothing should latch PROPSTRIKE_WORLD=gen5_plant python3 PropStrikePlugin/scripts/crash_battery.py landings \ /tmp/landings.json --n 3 --alt 8

Every cycle should report latched: false.
