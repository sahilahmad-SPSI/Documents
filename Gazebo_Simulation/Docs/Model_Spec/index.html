Specifications

What the model is

Converted from GEN5 V1 MAIN.step — your CAD, not an approximation of it. The airframe carries a 550 g Tritex UT kit and a forward probe boom with an orange tip, and it flies on ArduPilot SITL: the same autopilot firmware as a real aircraft.

Hover was predicted at 4 399 RPM from the bench table before the aircraft ever flew, and the simulator settled at exactly that. A blind prediction matched to the digit is worth more than any amount of after-the-fact tuning.

Construction

How the model was made

  1. Read the CAD, do not trust the box

    Dimensions come from probing the analytic STEP surfaces, never from a mesh bounding box. That discipline caught an 8.2 % oversized wheel radius elsewhere in the project; the same rule produced the propeller geometry here to a third of a millimetre.

  2. Decimate before scaling

    The mesh is simplified before the millimetre-to-metre conversion. Doing it the other way round wrecks the tolerances the decimator works to.

  3. Anchor the mass, then distribute it

    Total mass is pinned to the real 12.000 kg. Each link then carries its own mass and its own centre of mass, so the CG falls out of the geometry rather than being dialled in.

  4. Wire the autopilot around it, not into it

    The gen5 package is never modified. A wrapper model, gen5_with_ardupilot, includes it and adds the ArduPilot interface, an IMU, the FPV camera and the probe tip. The flight stack can change without touching the airframe.

  5. Model thrust where it actually acts

    Each rotor is three aerofoil blades at 120°, with the centre of pressure at radius 165 mm and +28.8 mm above the rotor origin — the propeller disc plane. The joint stays at the motor bell where the CAD puts it; the force is applied at the disc where the air is. Blade area and drag coefficient are then derived from the bench constants rather than tuned.

Balance

Where the centre of gravity actually is

The CG is not at the middle of the aircraft, and it is not supposed to be. The forward probe boom pulls it ahead of the thrust centroid — the point midway between the four rotors, which is where the lift effectively acts.

What that offset does in flight

An offset between CG and thrust centroid is a steady moment at hover, M = m g × offset:

pitch +0.8983 N·m # nose-down tendency from the forward probe roll +0.3896 N·m # for scale: one rotor at hover is 29.43 N # on a 0.2897 m arm = 8.53 N.m of authority

So the standing moment is about a tenth of what a single rotor can produce — comfortably inside the autopilot's authority, and it is trimmed out automatically. You can see it in the telemetry: the front motors run slightly faster than the rear, holding the probe boom up. That is the real aircraft's behaviour, reproduced.

Adjustment

How to change the centre of gravity

The CG is a consequence of three things: which links have mass, how much, and where each link's own centre of mass sits. Change any of those and the CG moves. There is no separate "CG setting" to edit — and that is deliberate, because it keeps the model honest.

The two files that matter

Models/converted/gen5/model.sdf # the airframe — base, probe, UT kit, rotors Gen5Flight/models/gen5_with_ardupilot/model.sdf # wrapper — IMU, camera, probe tip

What a link's mass block looks like

<link name="tritex_link"> <inertial> <pose>-0.0922293 3.84997e-05 -0.0285774 0 0 0</pose> # where this link's own CG sits <mass>0.5500</mass> # kg <inertia> ... </inertia> </inertial> </link>

Three ways to move it

  1. Move a payload — the honest way

    Change the <pose> inside a payload link's <inertial>. Shifting the 550 g UT kit backwards by 100 mm moves the whole-aircraft CG back by 0.550 × 0.100 / 12.016 = 4.6 mm. This is the one that corresponds to something you could actually do to the real machine.

  2. Add ballast — explicit and reversible

    Add a small link with its own mass at a chosen position and a fixed joint to base_link. It shows up in cg_check.py immediately and is trivial to remove again. Prefer this to quietly editing an existing link's mass.

  3. Re-trim the base link — last resort

    Editing base_link's inertial pose moves the CG fastest, because it is 82.8 % of the aircraft: 1 mm of base-link shift is about 0.83 mm of aircraft CG. It is also the least defensible, because that pose came from the CAD. If you change it, write down why.

The loop to work in

# 1. edit the mass or inertial pose # 2. see what it did — no simulation needed, this reads the SDF python3 Gen5Flight/scripts/cg_check.py # 3. prove it still flies: hover + rotor speed, against the recorded bars tools/regression/run.sh --full --only g3_gate0,g4_hover # 4. and that wind behaviour is unchanged tools/regression/run.sh --full --only g5_wind_lean
Changing the CG invalidates the flight results until you re-earn them

Hover rotor speed, the 5.010° wind lean and the chimney mission were all measured on this mass distribution. Moving the CG changes the trim, which changes the rotor speeds, which changes everything downstream. Re-run the gates before quoting any old number.

Two things not to do

Do not change the total mass to move the CG. 12.000 kg is anchored to the real aircraft; if the total drifts, every thrust and wind result loses its reference.

Do not edit the wrapper to fix an airframe balance problem. The wrapper exists so the flight stack and the airframe stay separable. Payload belongs in the airframe package.

A worked example

Say you want the CG on the thrust centroid in x — removing the 0.90 N·m nose-down trim. You need to shift 7.63 mm aft over 12.016 kg, a moment of 0.00763 × 12.016 = 0.0917 kg·m. Options:

  • move the 550 g UT kit aft by 0.0917 / 0.550 = 167 mm; or
  • add 250 g of ballast at 0.0917 / 0.250 = 367 mm aft of centre — which also pushes all-up mass to 12.27 kg, so the hover speed moves too.

The first costs nothing in mass and is what you would do on the real machine. The second is quicker to model but changes two things at once — and changing two things at once is how a result becomes impossible to attribute.
