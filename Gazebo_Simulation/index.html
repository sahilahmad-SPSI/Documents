What We Built

Six pieces, working together as one system. Everything below exists and has flown , nothing on this page is planned work.

Digital twin of the Gen5

Built from the real CAD files (STEP) of the actual machine. Its weight was put on a scale, its dimensions taken from the CAD , not guessed.

Real autopilot firmware

The simulated drone is flown by ArduPilot , the same autopilot software family that flies real aircraft. It runs unmodified (SITL: software-in-the-loop).

The real transmitter in your hands

The physical RC transmitter plugs into the PC and flies the simulation. Stick movements go through the same radio channels a real flight would use.

Industrial plant world

A simulated site with the striped inspection chimney as the mission target. A second, richer world was built from photographs of our own plant.

FPV camera + UT probe

A nose camera (1280×720) streams the pilot's view live. The orange-tipped ultrasonic probe sits visibly in frame, so probe contact can be seen as it happens.

Calibrated wind with gusts

Wind is a property of the world, controlled from a point-and-click panel: direction, speed, and breathing gusts. The drone leans into it, measurably.

The mission this enables: take off, fly to the chimney on the FPV view, touch the ultrasonic probe to the wall, hold contact, and return , in calm air or in gusting wind. This full mission has been flown successfully, repeatedly.

The propellers generate lift from the manufacturer's bench test data (thrust and torque measured on a real test stand), not from generic defaults. The UT probe is modelled at its true position on the nose, with its contact disc at the correct face , so a probe touch in simulation happens where it happens on the real drone. The drone also feels wind on its body, and its propellers can physically strike obstacles (see Crash Behaviour).

How Consistent Is It? , The Discipline

Two rules were enforced for the whole build, and they are what make the numbers below trustworthy.

Rule 1 , measure first, model second. No number was invented or hand-tuned to "look right." Mass came from a scale, geometry from CAD, propulsion from bench charts.

Rule 2 , predict blind, then check. Before each behaviour was first run, its expected value was calculated on paper from the measurements and written down. Only then was the simulation run, and the two compared. That is the honest test: the simulation was never tuned toward the answer.

How a Crash Behaves

Crashes are physical events with an honest boundary, stated plainly.

What happens: the propellers carry real collision geometry (the full disc each blade sweeps), so flying into the chimney or a structure is a genuine physical impact , the drone is stopped or thrown by the contact, falls, and tumbles under gravity. The autopilot then detects the upset exactly as it would on a real aircraft and disarms itself, printing Crash: Disarming in the console for everyone to see.

Mass matters, correctly: a light 1.5 kg drone in the same simulation gets flipped by an impact that the 12 kg Gen5 pushes through. That difference is real physics , momentum , not a scripted effect.

The honest limit: nothing breaks. The physics engine models rigid bodies , it has no structural failure, so propellers never shatter and arms never snap. A real Gen5 hitting a wall at speed would lose propellers; the simulated one keeps its shape. An intact aircraft after an impact is therefore not a survivability claim, and we do not present it as one.

How the Gust Wind Works

Wind belongs to the world, as in reality , it does not follow the aircraft's nose.

The physics

The force on the drone comes from the relative air speed , wind minus the drone's own motion. Fly downwind and the push weakens; hold position and the drone must lean into it. The strength was calibrated once, at a 10 m/s reference, against the drone's real drag estimate , then verified in flight (the 4.31° vs 4.2° row above).

The control panel

A point-and-click window: tick Enable wind, choose the direction it comes from, set the speed, press Apply. Tick Gusting for a breathing wind that swells and eases on a set period. STOP WIND always works. Wind ramps in over ~3 seconds , it arrives like weather, not a light switch.

Validated under wind: the full chimney-touch mission , approach, probe contact, hold, return , was flown in 5 m/s wind gusting ±3 m/s with 100% probe contact maintained. That is the recommended demo setting.

Run the Simulation

Four terminals, about 3 minutes from cold. Every command verified on the demo machine.

Pre-flight checklist , before anyone is watching

  • Transmitter dongle plugged into USB, transmitter switched on
  • Throttle stick fully DOWN , the aircraft refuses to arm otherwise
  • No leftover Gazebo or MAVProxy windows from earlier sessions
  • After starting the bridge (step 3): move every stick once , an untouched stick reports itself as centred no matter where it really is
1 Start the world  (terminal 1)
cd ~/gz_ws/src/GazebooAssets export GZ_SIM_RESOURCE_PATH="$PWD/Models/converted:$PWD/Gen5Flight/models:$PWD/InspectionYard/exports:$GZ_SIM_RESOURCE_PATH" export GZ_SIM_SYSTEM_PLUGIN_PATH="$HOME/gz_ws/build:$GZ_SIM_SYSTEM_PLUGIN_PATH" gz sim -r Gen5Flight/worlds/gen5_plant.sdf

Wait until the drone and the striped chimney are visible.

2 Start the autopilot  (terminal 2)
~/gz_ws/src/GazebooAssets/Gen5Flight/scripts/start_sitl.sh

Always use this launcher script , never sim_vehicle.py directly, or the transmitter is silently invisible. Wait for the STABILIZE> prompt.

3 Connect the transmitter  (terminal 3)
cd ~/gz_ws/src/GazebooAssets SDL_LINUX_JOYSTICK_CLASSIC=1 python3 Gen5Flight/scripts/rc_bridge.py --check

Two columns of matching numbers appear. Move each stick and confirm all four channels respond. Leave this running for the whole flight , closing it releases the controls.

4 Wind panel  (terminal 4 , optional until needed)
cd ~/gz_ws/src/GazebooAssets python3 Gen5Flight/scripts/wind_panel.py

For the FPV view: in the Gazebo window, ⋮ menu → search "Image Display" → add it → choose topic /gen5/fpv.

5 Fly  (type in the MAVProxy window, terminal 2)
mode stabilize arm throttle # ...fly on the sticks; when finished: disarm
If it goes wrongDo this
Sticks do nothing, everything else fineTerminal 2 was not started with start_sitl.sh , restart it that way
Bridge says "no joystick"Dongle unplugged or transmitter off , re-plug, re-run
Refuses to armThrottle not fully down, or wait longer for the autopilot to settle
Anything alarmingCtrl-C in the bridge window (releases controls), then mode land and disarm in MAVProxy

Straight answers, if the room asks

Does it crash like a real drone?
The impact, fall, and autopilot crash-detection are real. Structural breakage is not modelled , nothing fractures, and we say so rather than claim it.
Why does the simulation run slower than real time?
The autopilot and physics run in lockstep so control is exact; that caps speed at roughly half real time. It is a fidelity choice, not a graphics limit.
What is the note about the transmitter?
The transmitter dongle has a known hardware fault (spurious full-throttle bursts). A software guard blocks them , validated against captured data. The dongle is scheduled for replacement after the review.
