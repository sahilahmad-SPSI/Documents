SPSI R&D · Drone & Rover Ticketing · Plan v2.0

How a ticket moves

The agreed lifecycle, from the moment someone sees a fault to the moment the R&D Head closes it. Seven statuses, two review gates, and six ways a ticket can leave the straight line.

Who does what

Creator
Anyone active. Raises the ticket, describes the fault, attaches evidence. Takes no further action.
Dev Owner
Named per machine. Triages, assigns a worker, sets the target date, and reviews the finished work first. The only person who assigns.
Ops Owner
Named per machine. Watches only. Notified at creation and closure. Never acts in the system.
Assignee
The worker. Accepts or declines, does the work, may bring in a helper, submits the result with evidence.
R&D Head
The second and final gate. Approves and closes, or returns for rework with a new target date.
Admin
Manages people, machines and projects. Picks up tickets nobody will accept, and closes reviews that have stalled.

The straight line

01

Someone raises a ticket

Creator→NEW

Machine, activity, description, priority, evidence. A Drive folder is created for the ticket in the same breath.

The Dev Owner for that machine is emailed immediately, because they have to act. The Ops Owner and the creator get it in the daily digest, because they don't.

02

The Dev Owner assigns it

NEW→ASSIGNED

They pick a worker from the employee list and set the target date. Both are mandatory. The worker is emailed with a link to the ticket.

Branch — not a real fault

If it's a duplicate or not a defect, the Dev Owner says so with a justification and it goes straight to review. It still needs the R&D Head's signature to close — nothing closes without one.

03

The worker accepts

ASSIGNED→IN PROGRESS

They open the ticket and accept it. Accepting is what starts the clock.

Branch — the date is unrealistic

At the moment of accepting, and only then, the worker may propose a different target date with a reason. The Dev Owner approves it in one click. If they don't answer within 24 hours, the proposed date takes effect on its own and the log records it as auto-approved.

Branch — the worker declines

Declining requires a reason and sends the ticket back to the Dev Owner, who picks someone else.

Two declines on the same ticket — by anyone, not the same person twice — and it goes to the Admins. The Admin's assignment is final: the decline button is not shown.

Branch — nobody picks it up

After 24 hours with no accept or decline, the worker and the Dev Owner get a reminder and the ticket shows a red flag. It does not escalate — a reminder is enough.

04

The work happens

AssigneeIN PROGRESS

Troubleshooting notes are appended, never overwritten — the history of what was tried is as useful as the fix.

The worker can bring in a helper from the employee list. The helper can log findings and upload files, but cannot submit and is not accountable. The ticket stays the assignee's to close.

05

The worker submits the result

IN PROGRESS→REVIEW · DEV

Root cause, action taken, preventive action — and verification: what was re-tested, under what conditions, what happened. Mandatory free text plus at least one attachment. Submission is blocked without both.

The Dev Owner for that machine is emailed.

Branch — the worker is the Dev Owner

If the person who did the work is the machine's own Dev Owner, this gate is skipped entirely and it goes straight to the R&D Head. Reviewing your own work is not a review.

06

The Dev Owner checks the engineering

REVIEW · DEV→REVIEW · HEAD

The person who knows the machine reads the root cause and the verification first. Passing it on is a judgement that the fix is technically sound, not a formality.

Branch — not good enough

Return for rework with comments and a new target date. Back to the same worker, rework count up by one.

07

The R&D Head signs it off

REVIEW · HEAD→CLOSED

Approve and the ticket closes. The creator, the worker, the Dev Owner and the Ops Owner are all told.

Branch — not good enough

Return for rework, with comments and a new target date. The ticket goes back to the worker and the rework count goes up. There is no limit on rounds, but the count is visible.

Branch — the review stalls

A ticket sitting at either gate for more than five working days becomes closable by an Admin. The ticket is marked closed by Admin under the stall rule permanently, and the log records who did it.

This is the answer to "the Head is travelling" — not a standing Admin power, which would quietly become the fastest route and hollow out both gates.

The rules underneath

  • Two review gates, one signature. The Dev Owner checks the engineering; only the R&D Head closes a ticket — including "not a defect". An Admin closes only under the stall rule.
  • Nothing is ever deleted. Not a ticket, not a log line, not a person. Retired things are marked inactive.
  • Every action is logged with who, when, and from which status to which.
  • Email links go to the ticket, never straight to an action. You act on the ticket screen, signed in as yourself, so the system always knows who did it.
  • A machine with no owner cannot have a ticket raised against it. It simply doesn't appear on the form.

What this costs

  • A ticket that would have closed in two days now takes four. That is the price of the second pair of eyes, and it was chosen deliberately.
  • Two gates means two places a ticket can sit. The stall rule covers both, because a Dev Owner on site for a fortnight stalls a ticket exactly like a travelling Head.
  • Five working days is a setting, not a constant — an Admin can change it without touching code.
