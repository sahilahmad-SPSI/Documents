R&D Account Handover

Thirty-six steps from your test build to a live system owned by the R&D account. Nothing is copied — the system is rebuilt clean, because there is no data to preserve. Tick as you go; progress follows you between computers.

Checking where your progress is saved…

Do not duplicate your test sheet Making a copy of a Google Sheet copies its Apps Script project too. You would be pasting new code on top of a stale copy, and the copied Config tab would carry PHOTO_FOLDER_ID pointing at a folder in your Drive — so evidence photos would keep landing in your account while everyone believed the handover was done. A copy also brings across neither the trigger nor the deployment, so it saves nothing. Build fresh.
Part 1

Check these before you start

Step 1 is the one that can kill the whole thing Identity comes from the viewer's Google account, and that only works when the viewer and the script owner are on the same Workspace domain. If the R&D account sits on a different domain to your technicians, every one of them sees "Could not read your Google account" and nothing can be done in code to fix it. Confirm this before you spend an hour on the rest.
Step 1
The R&D account is on the same domain as the technicians
Its address ends @spsig.com, exactly like Suraj's and Naresh's. Not a personal Gmail, not a different company domain.
Step 2
The R&D account can send email
Sign in as it and send yourself one test email from Gmail. A no-reply or licence-less account cannot send, and every reminder in this system would fail silently.
Step 3
You have the seven real email addresses
Suraj, Praveen, Naresh, Inderjeet, Uttam, Naved, Abhishek — confirmed, not guessed from first names. Wrong addresses fail silently; the log will still say "sent".
Step 4
You have the seven files to hand
Five scripts — 00_Config.gs, 01_Setup.gs, 02_Api.gs, 03_Notify.gs, 04_Dashboard.gs — and two pages, Index.html and Admin.html. Open each in a text editor now so you can copy from them without hunting.
Part 2

Shut down your test build

Do this first. Leave your test running and the seven men get two sets of reminders from two systems, and you will spend a week working out which one is real.

Both steps here can legitimately find nothing to remove. An empty triggers list or an empty deployments list is the goal, not a problem — as long as you have confirmed you are in the right project, signed in as the account that built it.
Step 5
Open the script editor of your test sheet
Signed in as you, open your test spreadsheet.
Extensions ▸ Apps Script A new browser tab with your project and the file list on the left.
Step 6
Delete the daily trigger
In the narrow icon strip on the far left, click the alarm clock icon (Triggers).
Triggers ▸ hover the dailyTick row ▸ ⋮ (three dots, right-hand end) ▸ Delete trigger ▸ Delete forever An empty triggers list — "Showing 0 triggers".
Already showing 0 triggers? Then there is nothing to delete and this step is done — but check two things before you believe it. First, click the <> icon on the left: if you cannot see your seven files, you are in the wrong project, and the project name at the top will usually say "Untitled project". Reach it from the spreadsheet with Extensions ▸ Apps Script, never from script.google.com. Second, check the avatar top right — triggers are invisible to every account except the one that created them, which is why there is an "Owned by" column.
Step 7
Archive the old deployment
Top right of the script editor.
Deploy ▸ Manage deployments ▸ click the deployment row ▸ ⋮ ▸ Archive The deployment marked Archived. The old URL stops working — that is the point.
If the list is empty, you never deployed from this project and there is nothing to archive. Tick and move on.
Step 8
Rename the test sheet so nobody opens it by mistake
Back on the spreadsheet tab, click the file name at the top left.
Click the title ▸ type: OLD TEST — DO NOT USE ▸ Enter
Part 3

Sign in as the R&D account, in its own window
Use a separate Chrome profile, or an Incognito window. Two Google accounts in one window is how people end up creating the sheet under the wrong one and not noticing for a week.
Your avatar top-right showing the R&D account, and nothing else signed in.

Name the project
Click "Untitled project" (top, next to the Apps Script logo) ▸ type: Tritex & Herelink Maintenance ▸ Rename

Delete the empty Code.gs

Step 15
Add the five script files, one at a time
For each of 00_Config, 01_Setup, 02_Api, 03_Notify, 04_Dashboard:
Click + (next to "Files") ▸ Script ▸ type the name ▸ Enter Click in the code area ▸ Ctrl+A ▸ Ctrl+V
Type the name without .gs — the editor adds it. A new file arrives with a stub myFunction in it; Ctrl+A before pasting is what removes it.
Five files, named exactly as above, each starting with a /** comment block.
Step 16
Add the two pages
Click + ▸ HTML ▸ type: Index ▸ Enter ▸ Ctrl+A ▸ paste Index.html Click + ▸ HTML ▸ type: Admin ▸ Enter ▸ Ctrl+A ▸ paste Admin.html
These two names are not cosmetic — the server asks for templates called Index and Admin by name. A typo here shows up later as a blank page.
Seven files in total.
Step 17
Save
Ctrl+S  (or the floppy-disk icon in the toolbar) No red error underlines anywhere in the file list.
Part 4

First run and authorisation

Step 18
Reload the spreadsheet
Switch back to the spreadsheet tab and press F5. Wait about five seconds after it loads.
A new menu, Maintenance System, to the right of Help.
Step 19
Run setup — it will stop and ask for permission
Maintenance System ▸ 1. Run setup / repair
An "Authorization required" box appears. This is expected and it means setup has not run yet.
Step 20
Grant the permissions
Continue ▸ choose the R&D account ▸ "Google hasn't verified this app" ▸ Advanced ▸ Go to Tritex & Herelink Maintenance (unsafe) ▸ Allow
The scary wording is normal. "Unverified" means the script has not been through Google's review for public distribution — it is your own code, in your own account, and nobody outside the domain can run it.
Step 21
Run setup again — the first attempt was consumed by the permission prompt
Maintenance System ▸ 1. Run setup / repair A "Setup complete" box listing the tabs it created. Click OK.
Along the bottom you should now have eight tabs: Register, CheckLog, Checklists, Technicians, Config, Dashboard, SystemLog, ScheduleLog. The Register should hold 17 devices.
Part 5

Deploy the web app

Step 22
Start a new deployment
Switch to the Apps Script tab. The blue Deploy button is top right.
Deploy ▸ New deployment
Step 23
Choose the Web app type
Click the gear icon next to "Select type" ▸ Web app
Step 24
Set the three fields exactly
Description: v1 Execute as: Me (the R&D account address) Who has access: Anyone in Spsig ▸ Deploy
Execute as: Me is what lets the script write to the sheet and Drive on everyone's behalf without giving seven people edit rights. Anyone in Spsig is what lets it read their identity. Neither is optional.
Step 25
Copy the web app URL
The box shows a long URL ending in /exec. Copy it somewhere safe — this is the link your technicians will use.
Copy the "Web app" URL ▸ Done

Part 7

Prove it works before anyone relies on it

Spreadsheet ▸ Maintenance System ▸ About / health check "Health check — all good", or a list of exactly what is still wrong.

Console ▸ Schedule tab ▸ pick ONE device ▸ set its Owner to yourself ▸ set its Next service date to tomorrow ▸ Save changes Within a minute, an email titled "Equipment assigned to you: 1 item(s)". If it does not arrive, stop here and fix it — everything downstream depends on mail working.
Step 33
Open the app on your own phone
Paste the web app URL into your phone's browser, signed in as your own @spsig.com account.
Your name in the top right, a Scheduler button beside it, and the device you just scheduled under "Needs attention".
Step 34
Have one technician submit one real check
Send the link to Suraj or Naresh and ask him to record one device, with a photo.
Spreadsheet ▸ CheckLog tab ▸ check the new row His email in the "Submitted By Email" column — not yours. Click the Photo Link and confirm the image opens, and that it lives in the R&D account's Drive under "Maintenance Evidence Photos".
This single test proves identity, permissions, photo storage and the audit trail in one go. Do not skip it.
Part 8

Go live

Step 35
Set the remaining service dates
Console ▸ Schedule tab ▸ Fill suggested dates ▸ adjust ▸ Save changes
Stagger them. "Fill suggested dates" puts every device on the same day, which means one large mail to seven men on one morning and seventeen checks owed at once. Spread them across the month before you save.
Saving is what sends each man his first email. Do it when you actually want them to start.
Step 36
Send the link to the seven
Tell them to open it once and add it to their home screen — Chrome: ⋮ ▸ Add to Home screen. Safari: Share ▸ Add to Home Screen.
One thing worth doing afterwards If Spsig has a Shared Drive, move the spreadsheet and the Maintenance Evidence Photos folder into it. The organisation then owns both, and the next time somebody changes role nobody has to work through this checklist again. The daily trigger and the deployment would still need re-establishing once by whoever takes it on — those always follow the person, never the file.
