The Parallel Lane Problem

Why heading correction alone cannot hold a vertical lane, what the required angle accuracy actually works out to, and the control architecture that follows from it.

Three findings, each checked against ArduPilot source rather than documentation. The first two say the current approach cannot reach the required accuracy no matter how well it is tuned. The third says the fix is available to us, but we have to build it.

279 mm drift per 40 m pass

A sustained 0.4° tilt , the figure already raised in the thread , produces 279 mm of lateral drift over a full pass. Angle accuracy alone cannot close this.

0.014° needed for 10 mm over 40 m

That is 0.86 arcmin, sustained, for seven minutes. Not achievable from a MEMS accelerometer plus a mechanical mount.

Lua is the only path

ArduRover's built-in cross-track works in the horizontal plane only. On a vertical wall it degenerates. We have to implement the outer loop ourselves.

1The question being asked

Restated precisely: a controller that corrects heading returns the rover to the correct heading, but on a line parallel to the one it started on. The lateral offset accumulated during the excursion is never removed, because nothing in the loop measures it. Repeat that a few times over a 40 m pass and the rover is painting a different lane from the one it was commissioned to paint.

This has been asked four times in the thread and has not yet been answered. It is the correct question, and it has a clean answer.

2Why angle control cannot answer it

The arithmetic that settles it

Lateral drift for a sustained tilt θ over a travelled distance L is simply Δv = L · sinθ. Over a full 40 m pass:

Conclusion. The programme needs an absolute lateral reference roughly every 5–10 m of travel, or an overlap budget sized for the worst-case single-pass drift. Chasing tighter angle thresholds , 0.5° to 0.2° to 0.1° , buys a linear improvement against a requirement that is two orders of magnitude away. It is the wrong axis to optimise on.

3What ArduRover gives us, and what it does not

ArduRover already implements exactly the control in panel B , its waypoint navigation steers to a line between two waypoints, not to a heading, and actively drives cross-track error to zero. Two facts about it matter, and they pull in opposite directions.

First , it is off in ACRORover::get_wp_crosstrack_error_m() returns false unless control_mode->is_autopilot_mode(). That is true for AUTO, GUIDED, LOITER, RTL, SMART_RTL, CIRCLE, FOLLOW, DOCK and false for ACRO, MANUAL, HOLD, STEERING, SIMPLE. All bench and ramp testing so far has been in ACRO, where ArduRover holds a turn rate and nothing else. The parallel-lane behaviour we are seeing is the defined behaviour of that mode, not a sensor limitation.
Second , it will not transfer to the tankAR_WPNav::calc_crosstrack_error() computes orig.get_distance_NE(dest) into a Vector2f. It is a purely horizontal-plane calculation; altitude does not appear in it. A 40 m vertical climb produces near-zero North-East displacement, so on a vertical wall the track length collapses toward zero and the function degenerates. Rover's built-in cross-track cannot track a vertical line.

So AUTO mode is worth testing on the inclined ramp , it will demonstrate that the cascade principle works and that our wheel odometry is good enough to feed it , but the production solution has to reimplement the outer loop in the wall plane. That is a Lua job, and it is the reason the Lua capability review matters to this problem rather than being a parallel exercise.

Two more constraints worth knowing nowRover has no poscontrol binding , unlike Copter, we cannot inject a position offset into the running controller, so our script must own the outer loop outright. Rover also has no register_custom_mode(), so a "painting mode" has to be GUIDED with streamed commands, or a behaviour layer overlaid on an existing mode via an RC aux switch.

4The observation that makes this tractable

The single most useful result in the thread so far is Rohit's 13 August note that heading changes are being detected as roll changes. That is not an artefact , it is the geometry, and it is very good news.

On a vertical wall the rover's forward axis points along gravity. Rotation about the wall normal , exactly the quantity we care about , therefore appears directly in the gravity vector. That gives us an absolute, drift-free angular reference, which a ground rover normally cannot have without a compass. No gyro integration, no accumulating bias on the angle channel. It is the inclinometer principle the mechanical design already relies on, now available to the flight controller.

One caveat on how to read itAt pitch near ±90° the Euler decomposition is singular, so ahrs:get_roll_rad() and get_yaw_rad() become coupled and noisy. Do not build the loop on Euler angles. Rotate the body forward axis into the earth frame instead , ahrs:body_to_earth(Vector3f(1,0,0)), or take ahrs:get_quaternion() directly , and measure its departure from vertical. Both are singularity-free and both exist in the Lua binding set.

What gravity cannot give us is position. An absolute angle reference with zero drift still produces unbounded lateral drift, because the offset is the integral of the angle error over distance. That is the whole content of section 2.

5Proposed control architecture

Commanding is straightforward from Lua. vehicle:set_desired_turn_rate_and_speed() is a Rover-only binding and sits at exactly the right level , it leaves ArduRover's speed controller and slew limiting in place. vehicle:set_steering_and_throttle() is available if we need to bypass those, but it makes the script responsible for rate limiting and stopping, which on a tethered vehicle on a wall is a responsibility worth avoiding.

-- Outer loop sketch. Angle from gravity, offset by dead reckoning,
-- commanded through Rover's own turn-rate controller.
local RATE_MS = 20                         -- 50 Hz
local KP_V, KD_V = 0.8, 0.15              -- offset -> commanded tilt, to be tuned
local TILT_MAX = math.rad(6)                 -- never command more than 6 deg off vertical
local v_est, last_ms = 0.0, millis():toint()

local function tilt_from_vertical()
  -- body forward axis expressed in earth NED; singularity-free at pitch 90
  local fwd = ahrs:body_to_earth(Vector3f())    -- set x=1 before calling
  local up   = -fwd:z()                        -- NED down is negative up
  local lat  = fwd:y()                         -- lateral component, sign = drift direction
  return math.atan(lat, up)                   -- radians, signed
end

function update()
  local now = millis():toint()
  local dt  = (now - last_ms) * 0.001
  last_ms = now
  if dt <= 0 or dt > 0.5 then return update, RATE_MS end

  local theta = tilt_from_vertical()
  local speed = 0.0
  local vel   = ahrs:get_velocity_NED()
  if vel then speed = vel:length() end

  v_est = v_est + speed * math.sin(theta) * dt   -- accumulates; must be reset by a fix

  local theta_cmd = -(KP_V * v_est + KD_V * speed * math.sin(theta))
  if theta_cmd >  TILT_MAX then theta_cmd =  TILT_MAX end
  if theta_cmd < -TILT_MAX then theta_cmd = -TILT_MAX end

  local turn_rate = math.deg(theta_cmd - theta) * 2.0   -- inner P term
  vehicle:set_desired_turn_rate_and_speed(turn_rate, speed)

  logger:write("XTRK", "Th,Vest,Tcmd,Spd", "ffff", "----", "----",
               math.deg(theta), v_est, math.deg(theta_cmd), speed)
  return update, RATE_MS
end

return update, RATE_MS

Sketch only , gains are placeholders, the Vector3f needs its x component set before the frame conversion, and v_est has no reset path until section 6 is decided. It is here to show the shape of the loop and which bindings it uses, not to be flashed.

6The absolute lateral reference , options

Every option raised in the thread is assessed here on one criterion: does it observe lateral position, or only angle? Only the first group can bound the drift.

OptionObserves offset?Assessment
Re-datum at each end of the pass
mechanical stop, tether anchor, or operator-set marker		 yes Cheapest real answer, and not yet proposed. Bounds drift per pass instead of letting it accumulate across passes. On its own it needs the overlap budget to cover a full-pass worst case , so it pairs naturally with the next row.
Edge-follow the previously painted strip
line-scan sensor or camera on the painted side		 yes Strongest continuous option. The wet-dry paint boundary is a genuine high-contrast edge, which is a far easier detection problem than tracking texture. Mount it behind the spray in the direction of travel and on the already-painted side, which answers the lens-fouling objection. Fails on the first pass of a tank , covered by the row above.
Tether angle at the rover
two-axis encoder or pot at the tether entry		 yes The tether is already there and already anchored. Sensitivity is inverse to remaining tether length, so it is precise near the anchor and poor far from it , useful as a partial fix rather than a full-pass solution. Cheap enough to be worth prototyping.
Laser pointer plus receiver yes Optically feasible at 40 m with the right aperture. The real cost is operational: the pointer must be re-aimed for every lane change, on a tank. Rank below the three above for that reason, not on physics.
Optical flow partly Both objections raised in the thread stand: a uniform painted steel surface is feature-poor, sub-300 mm standoff restricts FOV, and paint will foul the lens. Not recommended as the primary reference. Edge-following gets the same information from a much easier signal.
Faster angle detection (0.5° → 0.2°) no Reduces the rate of accumulation, does not remove accumulated offset. Section 2 shows the requirement is around 0.014° over a full pass, so this is a linear gain against a gap of roughly 30×. Worth doing, cannot be the answer.
Gyro integration no The gyro's proper job here is rate damping in the inner loop. We do not need it for absolute angle , gravity already gives that without drift , and integrating it for position compounds two error sources instead of one.
Omni wheels no Provides lateral actuation, which is the half of the loop we already have , a skid-steer rover can correct laterally by tilting. It does not tell us that we have drifted. Significant mechanical change for the wrong half of the problem.
RecommendationPair per-pass re-datum with paint-edge following. Between them the first pass is bounded mechanically and every subsequent pass is bounded continuously, with no new failure mode introduced on the tank. Prototype the tether-angle sensor in parallel , it is nearly free and would give an independent cross-check.

7Next tests

Three experiments, in priority order. The first is the one that converts this argument from opinion into data, and it has not been proposed yet.

Test 1 , measure the actual drift distribution

On the ramp, at the steepest angle available. Mark a start line. Drive a fixed distance under the current ACRO heading-hold logic. Measure lateral offset at the finish. Repeat ten times. Plot the distribution.

This gives the number the overlap budget actually needs: worst-case drift per metre travelled. Everything in section 6 is a trade-off against that single figure, and right now nobody has it.

Test 2 , quantify the sustained angle bias

Run SAVE_TRIM , confirmed not yet done , then log tilt_from_vertical() for a full straight run at constant speed. The mean is our sustained bias; the standard deviation is our noise floor. Compare against the table in section 2 to read off how often we must re-datum. This single number decides whether per-pass re-datum alone is sufficient or whether edge-following is mandatory.

Test 3 , AUTO waypoint mission on the ramp

Two waypoints defining a straight line up the ramp, wheel encoders configured as the EKF velocity source, logging XTRACK. This proves the cascade works and that our odometry is good enough to feed an outer loop, before we spend effort reimplementing it in Lua.

Read the result carefullyA pass here validates the principle only. Section 3 shows this will not carry to the vertical tank, because the built-in cross-track is computed in the horizontal plane. Do not let a good ramp result close the question.

ArduPilot supports exactly two wheel encoders (WHEELENCODER_MAX_INSTANCES = 2), which for a skid-steer platform is precisely what is needed , one per side. The two-encoder limit noted earlier in the thread is real but not a constraint for us.

8Open questions

  • What is the actual overlap budget? The 10 mm figure has been used informally. Section 2's conclusions move roughly linearly with it, so it is worth pinning down before committing to a sensor.
  • How much wheel slip do we get on a painted magnetic surface? Dead reckoning is only as good as the speed estimate, and slip is invisible to an encoder. Test 1 will show it indirectly.
  • Does the EKF stay well-conditioned at pitch 90° over a long run? The quaternion is singularity-free, but ArduPilot's estimator is not designed for a vehicle that lives at the pole. Worth watching ahrs:get_variances() during Test 1.
  • Does the rover ever need to hold a lane while stationary? If it pauses mid-pass to paint, the dead-reckoning term stops updating but real drift from sag or slip does not. That changes the sensor requirement.
